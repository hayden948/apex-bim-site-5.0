The problem

Modeling content is where BIM schedules quietly bleed out.

Every family built by hand is hours your team isn't coordinating, and a chance for the model to drift from what gets installed.

◷

Hours lost to manual modeling

Detailers spend whole days redrawing geometry from PDFs that a manufacturer already published. Multiply that across every submittal and the calendar disappears.

▤

Inconsistent data & standards

Parameters, naming and LOD shift family to family and modeler to modeler. Schedules don't add up and downstream automation has nothing reliable to read.

⚠

Clashes found in the field, not the model

When clearance zones and connectors are missing, coordination passes clean and the conflict shows up on site — as an RFI, a delay and a change order.

Workflow

From drawing to family in five steps.

Drop in source content and APEX returns a coordination-correct family — every step is auditable, none of it is hand-modeled.

01

Upload shop drawing or submittal

Feed APEX a manufacturer PDF, submittal package or cut sheet. No template prep, no manual tracing — just the source you already have on hand.

02

AI reads geometry, tags & dimensions

Computer vision extracts overall geometry, dimension strings, connector locations and the typed data buried in tables and title blocks.

03

Builds a parametric LOD 400 family

APEX authors a native parametric family with driven dimensions and flex points — fabrication-level detail, not a placeholder box.

04

Embeds data, clearance zones & nested survey points

Electrical, mechanical and procurement parameters are written in, maintenance clearances are modeled, and survey points are nested for layout.

05

Validate & export field-ready content

Automated checks confirm geometry, data completeness and clearance before you ship a clash-ready, schedule-correct family into the project.

✦

Parametric, coordination-correct geometry

Families come out as true parametric content — driven dimensions, real connectors and accurate solids that flex to match the spec, not approximations a coordinator has to fight.
